Before the algorithm chooses anything, the city has to know where a tree can actually go. CoolSpend never optimizes over open space. It first builds a finite set of candidate slots — each one a single, pre-validated spot a tree could physically occupy. It starts on a four-metre lattice, then tests every point against the plantability constraints: inside the site, off the buildings, off the roadway, clear of every façade, and at least eight metres from any other tree. That eight-metre spacing isn't a guess — it's where three standards converge: NACTO's six-to-nine, the climate guideline's eight-to-ten, de-paving practice's six-to-ten. Then each slot picks a mode. A planter sits on pavement — contained roots, just point-six metres off a wall, no digging. In-ground means a de-paved pit, roots in real soil — a larger mature crown, so more cooling, but a six-metre foundation setback and a buried-utility risk the system won't pretend it can see. So every in-ground slot ships with one honest flag: requires utility survey.
